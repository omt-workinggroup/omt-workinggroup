Learning researchers have tested many study methods. A few consistently outperform re-reading and highlighting. A widely cited review is Dunlosky et al. (2013), Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, in Psychological Science in the Public Interest.

1. Retrieval practice (active recall)

Test yourself instead of re-reading. Close the book and write down what you remember, answer past questions, or use flashcards. The effort of recalling information strengthens memory.

2. Spaced repetition

Review material over several days and weeks rather than cramming the night before. For example, revise a topic one day, three days and one week after you first learn it. Flashcard apps such as Anki schedule this for you.

3. Interleaving

Mix different types of problems in one session (for example algebra, statistics and geometry) instead of doing twenty of the same kind. It feels harder but improves your ability to choose the right method in an exam.

4. Explain it in your own words

Ask yourself "why is this true?" and "how does this connect to what I already know?" If you can teach the idea to a friend simply, you understand it.

5. Focused study blocks

Study in focused blocks with short breaks; the Pomodoro Technique uses 25 minutes of work and a 5-minute break. Put your phone in another room.

A simple exam plan

  1. List every topic on the syllabus.
  2. Schedule each topic at least three times, spread out over the weeks before the exam.
  3. Start each session with a short self-test on the previous topic.
  4. Do at least one full past paper under timed conditions.
