Use the template

Download the DOCX and replace the bracketed text. Section headings use Word's built-in Heading 1 style, so changing the style once updates every heading.

What to put in each section

  • Header — name, phone number, a professional email address, city, and a LinkedIn or portfolio link if you have one. You don't need a photo, date of birth or marital status unless the employer asks for them.
  • Profile — two or three sentences: what you study, your strongest skills, and the role you want.
  • Education — most recent first: institution, programme, dates, and relevant results or projects.
  • Experience — internships, part-time jobs, volunteering, student societies and class projects all count. Start each bullet with an action verb and add a result: "Organised a 120-person career fair, raising attendance 40% over the previous year."
  • Skills — be specific: "Excel (PivotTables, XLOOKUP)" says more than "computer literate".
  • References — "Available on request" is fine unless the application asks for names.

Formatting rules

  • Keep it to one page while you are a student or recent graduate.
  • Use one readable font at 10–12 pt and consistent spacing.
  • Save and send as PDF (File › Save As › PDF) so the layout doesn't change on someone else's computer.
  • Name the file clearly: Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.

Before you send it

Tailor the profile and skills to each job advert, check spelling with Review › Editor, and ask someone to read it once.
