1. One idea per slide

Give each slide a headline that states its point, such as "Sales doubled after the redesign" instead of "Results". If you need two ideas, use two slides.

2. Fewer words, larger text

Slides support what you say; they are not your script. Aim for short phrases, and keep body text at least 24 pt so it can be read from the back of a room. Put the detail in the speaker notes.

3. Consistent design

Use one theme, two fonts at most, and a small colour palette. Edit the Slide Master (View › Slide Master) to change every slide at once.

4. Contrast and accessibility

Use dark text on a light background or the reverse. Don't rely on colour alone to show meaning, and add alt text to images (right-click › View Alt Text). Review › Check Accessibility flags common problems.

5. Honest, readable charts

Show one message per chart, label axes, start bar charts at zero, and highlight the bar that matters in your accent colour.

6. Use animation with purpose

Simple transitions such as Fade are enough. Use animation to reveal points one at a time when that helps people follow along, not for decoration.

7. Tell a story

A simple structure works for most talks: the situation, the problem, your solution and evidence, and what the audience should do next.

8. Rehearse

Use Slide Show › Rehearse with Coach in Microsoft 365 or practise with a timer. Know your first and last sentences by heart.
