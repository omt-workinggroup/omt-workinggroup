Every formula starts with =. In the examples, B2:B10 is a column of numbers such as test scores.

FormulaWhat it doesExample
SUMAdds numbers=SUM(B2:B10)
AVERAGEMean of numbers=AVERAGE(B2:B10)
MIN / MAXSmallest / largest value=MAX(B2:B10)
COUNTCounts cells containing numbers=COUNT(B2:B10)
COUNTACounts non-empty cells (text too)=COUNTA(A2:A10)
COUNTIFCounts cells meeting a condition=COUNTIF(B2:B10,">=50")

ROUNDRounds to a number of decimals=ROUND(AVERAGE(B2:B10),1)

Combining formulas

Formulas can sit inside each other. To give a grade from a score in B2:

=IF(B2>=70,"A",IF(B2>=60,"B",IF(B2>=50,"C","F")))

In Excel 2019 or Microsoft 365 the clearer IFS does the same thing:

=IFS(B2>=70,"A", B2>=60,"B", B2>=50,"C", TRUE,"F")

Relative and absolute references

B2 changes when you copy the formula; $B$2 stays fixed. Use absolute references for a single cell that every row needs, such as a pass mark stored in one cell: =IF(B2>=$F$1,"Pass","Fail").
